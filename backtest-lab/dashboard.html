Inside the champion

mechanics

Momentum Blend Band-22 is cross-sectional momentum with three refinements that earn their keep: a blended signal, a lazy exit, and a breadth-driven brake. On every month's last session it does four things:

STEP 1 / RANK

Blend three horizons

Every stock is ranked by its 3-month, 6-month and 12-month return — each skipping the most recent month to dodge short-term reversal — and the three percentile ranks are averaged into one score.

STEP 2 / GATE

Demand absolute momentum

A stock qualifies only if its 12-1 month return is positive. Relative winners in a falling market don't make the cut; their slots sit in cash instead.

STEP 3 / HOLD

Buy 10, sell at rank 22

The top 10 scores are held at 10% each — but an existing holding is kept until it decays past rank 22. The band cuts turnover roughly in half, and turnover is where backtests go to die.

STEP 4 / BRAKE

Scale out with breadth

When fewer stocks trade above their own 200-day average, the whole book scales toward cash (fully invested above 50% breadth, flat below 20%). Cash earns the liquid-fund rate. This is what turned 2008 from −27% into +2%.

Methodology & honesty

read before believing

◆No look-ahead

Signals use only each day's close; execution happens on the next bar. Stocks become eligible only after 252 bars of listed history.

◆Real NSE costs

25 basis points per side — brokerage, STT, stamp duty and impact — charged on all turnover. Idle cash earns 5% p.a. in a liquid fund.

◆IS / OOS discipline

All parameter choices (horizons, band width, breadth thresholds) were ranked on 2008–2017 in-sample data only. 2018–2026 was touched once, as validation.

◆Total-return data

Prices are split- and dividend-adjusted daily series for 49 current constituents, 2007–2026, sourced from Twelve Data (NSE).

Survivorship bias, declared: the universe is today's Nifty 50. Companies that fell out of the index along the way are missing, which flatters absolute returns for the strategies and the buy-and-hold benchmark alike — relative comparisons are the fair read. Past performance is not a promise; this is research, not investment advice.
